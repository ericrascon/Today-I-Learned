# What are accruals for?    

An accrual is a type of journal entry that records the revenues and expenses of the prior month that have not been received or paid out.

There are many types of accruals, but these 4 tend to be the most common.

### Examples

1. Expense accrual for interest: Accrued interest is the accumulation of an interest expense since it's last payment.
2. Expense accrual for supplier goods and services: A supplier provided a service or delivered goods on July 25th but didn't send the invoice to get paid until August 1st.
3. Expense accrual for wages: If an employee is paid monthly, from July 1st to July 26th the employee works, and payroll will pay/record those wages. From July 27th to July 31st the employee will also work and will also earn those wages howevery they will not be paid officially until September.
4. Sales accrual: An organization hires a third party for the completion of a big project; the contract states that the third party won't be paid the agreed amount, which is $100,000, until the project is finished. Let's say the third party has finished 40% of the project; they would then record a revenue accrual of $40,000.

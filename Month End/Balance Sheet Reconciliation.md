# What is a balance sheet reconciliation?

Not to be confused with a **bank reconciliation**, which specifically reconciles cash accounts to a bank statement, a **balance sheet reconciliation** verifies that the balances of the balance-sheet accounts in the General Ledger are accurate and supported.

This typically involves reconciling **asset, liability, and equity accounts** to their underlying supporting documentation or subsidiary records. Examples include:

- **Accounts receivable (AR)** → reconcile the GL balance to the AR subsidiary ledger.
- **Accounts payable (AP)** → reconcile the GL balance to the AP subsidiary ledger.
- **Fixed assets** → reconcile the GL balance to the fixed asset register and supporting schedules.
- **Loans** → reconcile the GL balance to lender statements and amortization schedules.
- **Cash** → reconcile the GL balance to bank statements through a bank reconciliation.
- **Accrued liabilities** → reconcile the GL balance to supporting accrual schedules and calculations.

In other words, **reconciling the balance sheet is the process of comparing the balances recorded in the GL for balance-sheet accounts against the supporting documentation or subsidiary records that substantiate those balances, investigating differences, and making corrections when necessary.**
